Why Mirai

A launcher you can actually look inside

Most Android launchers hand you a wrapper library and a prayer. Mirai is built so that when something breaks, you can find out why.

🧩

Renderers built from source

LTW and LTW Legacy compile from vendored sources in this repository, for every ABI. A wrong-architecture library is caught in CI, not after you press Play.

🎯

It picks the right wrapper

A renderer only runs on a Minecraft version it declares support for. The boundary is the OpenGL profile, not a version number nobody agrees on.

⚡

Tuned for a phone

A one-tap FPS preset, an automatic JRE and GC tuner, a crash reader and a mod conflict resolver — all applied per device and kept across restarts.

🛡️

Private by default

No ads. No telemetry. No account resale. Your worlds and your accounts stay on your device unless you choose a login that needs the network.

👤

Offline accounts, dressed up

Create a local username, then equip a skin and a cape on it — with a 3D preview you can spin before you launch.

🌍

Discover built in

Browse and install modpacks, mods, resource packs, shaders and saves. Downloads land in the instance you meant, not in a random folder.

Renderer stack

The part that decides whether it starts

Minecraft on Android is a translation problem. A wrapper turns the desktop OpenGL calls the game makes into whatever your device actually offers — and picking the wrong one is the single most common reason a version refuses to launch.

Mirai ships eleven of them, picks one for you based on the version and your device, and lets you override it per instance. Two of them are compiled from source right here.
